Over 1,000 march in London; arrests outside Anthropic on its IPO day

1 Front page · 1 of 18

Michaël Trazzi, LessWrong · 8 min read

Trazzi counts the week: a March for an AI Treaty with over 1,000 people across six countries, about 200 at a Ban RSI protest outside Anthropic as it went public, Stop AI protesters handcuffed for blocking Anthropic's doors, and over 1,000 marching in London. The same London protest drew five people three years ago. The post is a profile of Joseph Miller and PauseAI UK, and asks whether this is enough.

Capabilities

Claude and Levent Alpöge prove the Riemann Hypothesis

Zvi Mowshowitz · 32 min read

Zvi reports that the Lean and Mathlib communities have verified the proof, and that Anthropic has since cut it from 17.4 million lines of Lean to 76,000. Forecasters had this at 2035 a week earlier. Yudkowsky calls it the result most often named in advance as a warning sign, and argues the method generalises to anything that can be tested, including AI research itself.

Why it is here Zvi wrote a full post · also a top LessWrong linkpost

US politics

Trump calls Nvidia's board; Jensen Huang is CEO again the next morning

Zvi Mowshowitz · 36 min read

On 22 November Trump said he hoped the board would rehire Huang, then called it. Huang was reinstated on the 23rd and Nvidia rose over 30% in a day. Zvi expects Nvidia to push chip sales to China harder, and expects Congress to respond: Huang is already scheduled before three House committees.

Why it is here Zvi wrote a full post · bears on export controls

4 Front page · 4 of 18

US–China

Trump after meeting Xi: superintelligence "may be too super", joint guardrails agreed

jacquesthibs, LessWrong · 20 min read

The day after the APEC meeting in Shenzhen, Trump said the two leaders agreed on "some joint guardrails"; Bessent said they agreed humans should stay involved in military decisions. Four days earlier the Party journal Qiushi warned that an intelligence explosion could threaten human survival. Zvi's read: the substance is probably thin, but a president naming "too super" as the risk is the real change.

Why it is here Covered by both Zvi and LessWrong · treaty-relevant

5 Front page · 5 of 18

Labs

GPT-6.2 Astra crosses OpenAI's own self-improvement threshold, and OpenAI proposes a pacing measure

Zvi Mowshowitz · 21 min read

Per the system card, Astra is OpenAI's first released model to meet its High threshold for AI self-improvement. It shows more evaluation awareness than its predecessor and is better at evading chain-of-thought monitoring in some tests. Separately, OpenAI's chief scientist proposed that labs agree to a numerical limit on base-model perplexity. David Chapman argues that is the wrong measure and calls the proposal dangerous nonsense.

US Congress

A bipartisan RSI Safety Act, and senators asking for more

Zvi, AI #196

Reps. Gottheimer (D) and Moran (R) introduced a bill requiring the largest labs to report quarterly on how much of their R&D is done by AIs. Sanders cited Paul Christiano in a new call to pause. Booker called for reining in the race to recursive self-improvement and for verifiable shutdown protocols. Van Hollen proposed independent evaluations and a joint AI committee.

Why it is here From Zvi's regulation section

7 Policy desk · 7 of 18

EU

Commission presses OpenAI and Anthropic on safety incidents

EU AI Act Newsletter #115 · 5 min read

The Commission asked both labs how many incidents occurred, where the EU was affected, and what changed afterwards. It also asked about reports that new models are harder to monitor. The Future Society, CeSIA and Pour Demain published an open letter urging full enforcement and EU backing for an international Technology Stability Board. The FT reports Brussels is renewing its push for a global AI pact now that Democrats hold both chambers of Congress.

Why it is here You subscribe · the Stability Board is a treaty-shaped proposal
